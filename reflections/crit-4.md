# Crit 4 — Roundhouse

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

I had the loop running and I still didn't trust it. Then I played the built
page myself and heard hits I hadn't made — the instrument replaying my own
groove, which is exactly what I asked it to do, and I read it as a fault. That
was the moment the work turned. The problem was never the sound; it was that a
replayed hit looked almost nothing like a live one. The ring that leaves your
finger on a strike was only ever drawn on the live path, and the playhead was
driven from the scheduler's clock, so the line crossed a tick about fifty
milliseconds before you heard anything. Both were found by measuring the
running page, not by re-reading the code, which is what I would have done first
if my own instrument hadn't surprised me.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a developer?

I came in treating a green check as the finish line. This week it lied to me in
both directions: a graded assertion passed on a library I had merely
downloaded, and a value I had pinned in my own plan turned out to fail contrast
for three of the four pad colours once someone measured the rendered pixels
instead of trusting the arithmetic. I want to be the kind of developer who asks
what a passing check actually read, and who then writes the answer somewhere it
holds — a rule in `CLAUDE.md`, a test of my own — rather than fixing the line
and moving on. Directing agents made that unavoidable. Anything I only said
once was gone by the next task.
